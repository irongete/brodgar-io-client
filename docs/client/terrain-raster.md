# Which ground is drawn: the terrain rasters

> `MapView`'s terrain display lists — the one bolted to the player, the ones over another cache, and what
> bounds each. What is drawn *on* that ground is the ground-overlay half of [world-3d.md](world-3d.md).

| What | Where |
|---|---|
| **Which ground is DRAWN — the terrain display list** | `MapView.MapRaster` (private inner) → the public `Terrain` and its `Grid main`/`flavobjs`. `MapRaster.tick` — the base default, which `Terrain` takes and every other raster overrides — sets `area = Area(cc - view, cc + view + 1)` around the player's CUT (`getcc().floor(tilesz).div(MCache.cutsz)`, `view = 2`, `cutsz = 25×25` tiles) ⇒ the drawn terrain is only **~50–75 tiles** across from you; `Grid.tick` then adds/removes one scene slot per cut, keyed in `Grid.cuts`, which therefore holds a cut **exactly while that cut's mesh is in the scene**. ⚠️ That is a GRID smaller than `MCache.grids`: map data is dropped only when the server says so (`invalblob` type 1 → `MCache.trim`, which has **no caller inside the client**), so "the grid is loaded" is true well past the visible edge — test `cuts` when the claim is about what the player SEES. Fork: `MapView.grounddrawn(Coord2d)` (`// addon:`) + a `groundChanged()` tap at `Grid.tick`'s two mutation points, which is how a client-only gob stops drawing over the void. That tap is gated on `MapRaster.liveground()`, true by default and overridable: `grounddrawn` reads `terrain.main.cuts` **alone**, so a raster whose cuts are not that ground wakes the addon layer's placement pass for a set it never reads |
| **From a grid's arrival to the first drawn frame (fork)** | The black "Waiting for…" screen is `MapView.draw` catching the `Loading` that `camera.tick` → `getcc()` → `Gob.getc` → `MCache.getz(SurfaceID, …)` → `Grid.getcut` throws while the cut under the player has no mesh — its text is that cut's *last* stumble (`Defer.NotDoneException` carries `Future.lastload`, cleared only on completion), so it names a resource long since loaded; the DURATION is the wait for that one mesh, then the first drawn frame. Measured at login (1782–1797 ms of black, then a first frame of ~1.1 s) and taken apart: **`MCache.Grid.fill` → `Cut.invalidate()` is lazy** — `Deferred.invalidate()` drops the future in flight and clears `inited` (volatile: `get()`'s unlocked fast path tests it) so the next `get()` rebuilds; it used to `rebuild()` at once, scheduling a future for every cut of every grid the server sent (288 for nine grids, before the view had asked for its 25) and the first `get()` then scheduled each wanted cut *again*, so every cut on screen was built twice and the one under the camera queued behind the flood. **The cut under the player is urgent** from whoever asks first — `Gob.getc` for `Glob.plgob`, a `Loader` thread placing the player's own gob at login, before the camera has ticked — and the pool holds ordinary work while it is outstanding ([boot-and-loop.md](boot-and-loop.md), the urgent lane); its texture chain is discovered a stumble at a time (tileset → `-tex` → `transitions-*` → `ridges/*` → the paving `-tex`s under the character), each restart a full rebuild, which is what the login warm-up pre-loads. **The black frames do the first frame's setup**: `MapView.draw` runs `PView.ensureenv` (the instancer attached to an EMPTY tree — attached on the first drawn frame it walked every slot added meanwhile, compiling each and blocking on each unprepared texture through `RenderList.syncadd`'s `waitfor`: 720–1170 ms) and `lights()` (the light-grid classes and shaders, and a light state set *before* the terrain's slots, which a state set after recompiled every one of them), and `updsmap` makes the `ShadowList`/`ShadowMap` before asking `getcc()`, so shadow slots compile one by one as cuts arrive rather than as one walk on the first tick with a camera. A cut's `slot.add` that throws for a texture boosts it to `URGENT` before the per-frame retry. ⚠️ `Terrain.tick` re-asks only the cuts in `vis`, and `vis` comes from `cutvisible` over a camera that has not ticked — a cut outside that guess sits rescheduled until the camera is right; `reqarea`'s 49 `getcut`s run after `super.draw` and never during the black screen. After all of it: black 300–550 ms, of which the grids' own arrival ~130, a cut alone with warm code 19–50 ms, and the rest first-time class loading on the UI thread (`OCache.ctick`'s first pass over every gob, the widget flood the `Loader` applies under `synchronized(ui)`, the first draw's resource code) — the JVM's cold start, not the terrain's |
| **A SECOND source of ground in one scene** | Fork: `MapView.RecallTerrain extends MapRaster` over a second `MCache` filled from the map database ([mapfile.md](mapfile.md)) instead of from the wire — the same pattern as `MapView.SessionTerrain`, which rasterizes another *session's* `MCache` into this scene. What separates such a raster from `Terrain`: its `area` is centred on **the camera** (`RTSCam.center()`, or `getcc()` under every other camera, where the two are the same place), and is sized in whole GRIDS, because a grid is the unit its source reads. It **decides what that source reads** — `RecallTerrain.tick` builds the wanted grid set and `MapView.recalltick` hands it over to the source — so the one owner of that decision is the one that knows where the camera is pointing and what of that is on screen. What it holds is the set of grids `RecallTerrain.grids` is handed each tick by the far ground that draws the zoom grids ([mapfile.md](mapfile.md#zoom-grids)) — the level-one zoom cells close enough to the camera's eye to be drawn whole in any view a turn of the camera about its centre gives, as many as a share of the heap holds, nearest first — on screen now or not, so a turn of the camera hands over the same set —, every cell within `MapView.recallnear` cuts of the live area, and the cells still drawn whole while what replaces them is built — and what it draws is the part of it `RecallTerrain.shown` names; the rest it builds and merges out of the scene. What the source fills is one grid wider: `MapMesh.dotrans` reads a tile across the cut edge and the corner heights need the same tile, so a cut at the fill's own edge throws `MCache.LoadingMap` and never completes. What the source holds follows the record: a grid it holds that the record takes in again is read again and, where the ground changed, filled in place (`AddonWidgets.putgrid`), so each cut goes on drawing its old mesh until the new one is built and the grid is merged again as they land; a copy kept as read hands changed ground back as it was the moment the character walks away from it ([mapfile.md](mapfile.md), a copy read out of the record). It draws only grids the source is already holding (`AddonWidgets.loadedGrid`), never through a `getcut` that would ask for one. It yields every cut the live raster has **in the scene** (`terrain.main.cuts`), because both sources hold the same ground there and one mesh on its twin is z-fighting, not a merge — the same rule `SessionTerrain` obeys against the anchor — and it builds and holds the cuts it yields all the same. ⚠️ Yielding the live `area` instead, with none of it built, opened a hole on both edges of every step the character took: `Terrain` adds a cut only once its mesh is built (`reqarea` builds the ring one cut past `view` ahead of time) and drops one the moment it leaves `area`, so the recorded side let go of a cut the live side had not drawn yet, and needed a build to draw a cut the live side had just dropped; the sky showed through for a few frames. `RecallTerrain.mergetick` makes a grid whose live share changed again in that same tick, past its budget, and `Terrain` ticks first (`MapView.tick`), so a cut changes hands within one frame. Every grid it is handed is built, on screen or not, and the main draw list's frustum culling leaves out what is out of view; its slot carries `ShadowMap.maskshadow`, so none of it is drawn into the shadow map. ⚠️ A grid left unbuilt for being out of view is drawn whole with nothing in it the moment a turning camera reaches it. And it answers `liveground()` false. Installed in `basic` under every camera while `MapView.recallon` is on; removed with it, and whenever the source cannot vouch for the offset its ground was read through. ⚠️ Whatever takes such a raster out of the scene has to zero what its tick published for anything outside it, in the same breath: `MapRaster.Grid.tick` returns at once while `slot` is null, and a `MapView` that stops calling the raster's own `tick` is not calling it either, so a count or a wanted set left over from the last frame it was drawn stands for as long as it stays out. `Grid.cuts` is the one thing that empties itself (`Grid.removed`), which is what makes a stale figure beside it read as an honest zero. It is drawn in its tilesets' own materials, so nothing tells it apart from live ground but that nothing stands on it. ⚠️ Such ground is far outside the shadow map's box, and upstream's `ShadowMap.Shader.shcalc` shaded everything outside that box as in shadow ([render-gl.md](render-gl.md)) |
| **Where such a raster's settings live** | Fork: `MapView.recallon` and `MapView.recallrange` — two `static` fields with like-named prefs, each written live-and-persisted in **one statement** the way `MapView.invcamx` is, so neither half can move without the other. They are `static` because the settings are the **client's**: every session up draws its own remembered ground out of its own record, and a switch the user flips once has to move all of them. `MapView.recallrange` is the DRAWN reach in grids and the source is filled one grid wider for `dotrans`'s margin, so the raster's own `r` is the field and the fill's radius is the field plus one; both are read per tick, which is what makes a range change apply with nothing to rebuild and nothing to tell. `MapView.recallrangemin`/`recallrangemax` are the bounds, stated once and read by the Performance panel's `HSlider`, the field's own clamp and the Lua refusal; the slider starts at `recallrangepanel`, and a client with no stored range starts at `recallrangedef`. `MapView.recallreach()` is how far from its centre that ground can stand in world units, which `DefaultCam.setproj` holds its far plane past while `recallon` is on ([camera.md](camera.md)). `:recall` in `cmdmap` takes **no argument** and is a report: `MapView.recallgridsheld`/`recallgridsread`/`recallcutsdrawn`/`recallcutswanted` (`// addon:`) are the four numbers it prints, and the same four an addon reads back |
| **Bounding what such a raster DRAWS** | Fork: `MapMesh.build` is two passes over 625 tiles plus `dotrans`'s eight neighbour reads each, then a slot compile and a VBO upload, on the `Defer` threads every loading thing in the client shares, and `MapRaster` has no budget of its own. `Grid.tick` calls `getcut` for every cut `MapRaster.walk()` names, in **iteration order**, and drops the slot of every cut `MapRaster.holds` refuses — `area` and `area.contains` by default (`// addon:`). A raster whose cuts are a sparse set overrides both: the recorded ground's grids lie around the character and around where the camera looks, which can be a hundred grids apart, and the rectangle around both is a million cuts to walk every tick. `RecallTerrain.tick` takes every grid it is handed that the source holds (`AddonWidgets.loadedGrid`) **whole**, with no frustum test per cut: the ground is drawn a grid at a time (the next row), so a cut test would only change which cuts a visible grid is made of on every turn of the camera — a copy of the grid each time — and the main draw list's frustum culling tests the grid's mesh as one box instead. What is handed over is every grid some turn of the camera draws whole, and the tick runs **every ctick**, with no clock of its own — and does nothing at all when it finds what it was handed, the live cuts, `MCache.chseq` and the ground generation where they were and its last tick left nothing to do (`RecallTerrain.settled`; the click raster follows its `idle`), which is every tick of a camera standing or turning. A whole tick while the camera pans or zooms over grids held whole redoes only what moved: the candidate cuts (every cut of a grid handed over that the source holds and can build) when the grids handed over or the source's `MCache.chseq` move, `draw` when the candidates move or the build budget still holds some back, the walk of every cut held only when a cut of `draw` is missing from the scene or a mesh was built within `IDLEWAIT` (`RecallTerrain.maincurrent`), and `mergetick`'s pass over every grid only when its cuts, the live ones, what is shown, drawn or merged moved since a pass found every grid merged — `MapRaster.Grid.gen` (`// addon:`) moves at every change to a raster's `cuts`; `RecallClick` skips its own walk on the same terms, and the wanted and held grid sets handed to the source are made anew only when they change, so `Recall.want` finds the very same objects and returns. ⚠️ A cut invalidated by a refill or a ground setting goes on answering its OLD mesh from `getcut` until its rebuild lands, so a raster that stops asking never sees the new one: `MCache.meshbuilt` (`// addon:`) counts every finished cut build, and whole ticks, each walking every cut held, follow each for `IDLEWAIT` — a window and not the one tick that first sees the count, since the count moves inside `build()`, before the build's future is done, and a walk on that tick is still answered the old mesh. The cuts are sorted by distance from the camera's own cut and admitted until `recallcutcap(n)`, every cut of the `n` grids handed over: a cut left out is a hole nothing else fills. `draw` keeps that order and is what the raster walks. ⚠️ `boxvisible(ul, br)` tests a box at the **character's** height, `cc.z ± 100`: ground far below or above it (the character on a hill, a tower) projects elsewhere on screen, so a camera zoomed in or tilted down drops boxes that are in view. ⚠️ **A cut that can never build blocks every other one.** Its mesh reads one tile past each edge (`MapMesh`'s `Scan(-1, -1, sz + 2)`), so a cut on the edge of explored ground reads into a grid the record never had and throws `LoadingMap` for good; counted as in flight it holds a slot of `recallmaxbuild` forever, and once the budget is full of them nothing else starts. `RecallTerrain.buildable` admits only a cut whose grids are all held. ⚠️ A cap derived from the frustum instead is not a cap: the camera can frame more ground than this client was built to draw |
| **Drawing it a grid at a time (fork)** | A cut holds a mesh per material its tiles are laid in (`MapMesh.Model`, one per `MatKey`, and a `Tiler.SModel` per material and vertex factory), a few hundred triangles each, and every one is a draw call: hundreds of recorded cuts on screen were most of the scene's draw calls. `MapMesh.parts()` (`// addon:`) hands out each `Model`'s finished mesh with its material — never the `Model` itself (the gotcha on what a built cut keeps, below). `RecallTerrain.main` still holds every cut in the scene — the authority `heldgrids`, the budgets and `RecallClick` read — but its `produce` is `RenderTree.Node.nil`; `RecallTerrain.mergetick` draws each grid with cuts in `main` as the concatenation of those cuts' meshes — all but the ones the live raster has in the scene — per equal material and equal vertex layout, each cut's positions moved by where it stands in the grid, placed at the grid's corner. That mesh keeps **no copy** of what it draws: its vertices and indices are written from the cuts' own meshes straight into the buffers the GPU is filled from, when it is prepared and again if another environment ever asks (`FastMesh(VertexBuf, int, Filler, Volume3f)`, `// addon:` — no index array of its own, and the box given, which the frustum test reads), so a grid drawn whole holds its data once, in its cuts. A grid is merged again when its set of cuts changes: at once when every cut `draw` gives it has arrived, else once the set has stood still for `MERGEWAIT`, at most `MERGES` grids a tick, and at once past that budget when the live raster has just taken a cut of it or given one back; a grid waiting draws its previous mesh, which owns its buffers and holds on to the cut meshes it was made from, rebuilt since or not. A mesh whose material texture is not prepared (`Loading` on the add) is kept and added again next tick, not made anew. ⚠️ The indices are 16-bit: a group past 65,535 vertices starts another mesh. ⚠️ A mesh is placed by its slot's `Location`, and a copy's vertices are grid-local, so anything that reads object space (`Homo3D.objv`) would see the grid's frame instead of the cut's; the ground's materials read `mapv`, which is the same either way |
| **Bounding what it BUILDS, which is a different budget** | Fork: `MapView.recallmaxbuild` is a **concurrency target** and not a quota per tick — `Math.max(2, Defer.maxthreads - recallbuildreserve)`, a stated share of the pool that does the building ([boot-and-loop.md](boot-and-loop.md)). It is charged only for a cut that is in neither `Grid.cuts` nor `MCache.cutbuilt` (`// addon:`), so what it counts is what has been started and has not yet arrived, and a cut already built is admitted for free: `continue` past the budget, never `break`, or a run of unbuilt cuts stops the walk before it reaches the built ones behind them. Paired with a tick on the frame rather than on a period, throughput is that target over one build's own **latency** instead of over a clock — a finished build is replaced within a frame, where a fifth of a second left a five-millisecond mesh's slot in the budget idle for the rest of it |

**Gotcha — a raster over a cache the client must not send for cannot use `getcut` alone.** `MCache.getcut`
ends in `getgrid`, which on a miss calls `request(gc)` and throws `LoadingMap` — so a `MapRaster` walking an
area wider than its source has filled queues every absent grid in it. On the live cache that is the point; on
one filled from disk it fills a queue nothing may ever send, and buries the request count that is the only
evidence such a source is behaving. Ask the cache what it holds first (`AddonWidgets.loadedGrid`, `// addon:`)
and skip the cut when it holds nothing. That is also what drops a cut whose grid has just been disposed:
`MapRaster.Grid.tick` removes the slot of any cut its `skipcut` starts refusing, so the stale mesh leaves the
scene at the next tick instead of being drawn after its `dispose()`.

**Gotcha — a second source of ground is a second `MCache`, and only one of the two has a monitor anybody
else wants.** `AddonWidgets.loadedGrids(mc)` (`// addon:`) copies a cache's whole grid map and
`AddonWidgets.loadedGrid(mc, gc)` looks one coord up in it; **both take `mc.grids`**. On a raster's own
disk-filled cache that monitor is taken by the raster and by whatever fills it and by nothing else, so a copy
there is merely wasteful. On `sess.glob.map` it is the monitor the map and the render threads take, which
[world-3d.md](world-3d.md) already prices per cut. ⚠️ So a question about a handful of grids is asked **by
coord**, once per grid named — the same shape `RecallTerrain.tick`'s per-grid pre-reject already has.
Copying the session's cache to answer one pays that monitor for every grid the session has ever loaded, and
that set only grows: `MCache.trim` has no caller inside the client. The copy is also the smaller half of what
it costs, because the walk over it is a `Coord` and a lookup for each of those grids, on every pass.

**Gotcha — what such a source keeps is an LRU, and the raster's own `cuts` is what it may never drop.**
`MCache.trim` keeps a rectangle, and a rectangle cannot say *keep what was built*: one grid of travel puts a
whole rank of grids outside the square, so panning one way and back re-reads and re-meshes every one of them.
`MCache.drop(Collection<Coord>)` (`// addon:`) disposes exactly the grids it is handed instead, and it names
what **goes** rather than what stays — the keep set is decided on the UI thread while grids arrive from a
`Defer` thread, and naming what stays would dispose one read off the disk before anything could draw it.
⚠️ Order the two sides the right way round: an `MCache.Grid` disposes its cut meshes with itself and
`MapRaster.Grid` holds a scene slot per cut it has drawn, so **the raster's cut map is the authority over the
budget** — a grid under one of its entries is kept whatever the order says, or its slot goes on drawing a
disposed mesh with nothing to notice. `trimall` has no next tick to notice either: it disposes every `Grid` at
once, and a raster removed in the same breath never ticks again, so **the slot comes out first and the dispose
follows**.

**Gotcha — `skipcut` runs OUTSIDE `Grid.tick`'s `Loading` guard, and nothing above it catches one
either.** `Grid.tick` wraps only the `getcut` half in `try`/`catch(Loading)`; the `skipcut` test sits ahead
of that block, so a `Loading` out of it leaves `tick` altogether. There is no net under it: `MapView.oltick`
ticks its overlay rasters in a loop *after* its own `catch`, and `MapView.tick` calls `oltick` inside
`synchronized(glob.map)` with no guard of its own — so the throw takes the whole frame. A `skipcut` that
consults the cache (`MCache.getgrid`, `Indir.get`) must therefore **swallow `Loading` and answer
conservatively**: yield nothing, and let the `getcut` that follows throw where it is caught.

**Gotcha — a cut's MESH outlives the slot that drew it, so "not in `Grid.cuts`" never means "needs
building".** `MapRaster.Grid.removed` clears `cuts` when the raster leaves the tree, and `Grid.tick` drops
the slot of every cut `skipcut` refuses — neither touches `MCache.Grid.Cut.mesh`, a `Deferred` that holds
its built value until the whole `Grid` is disposed. So a raster that came out of the scene and went back in
finds every cut it had built still built, and `Deferred.get` hands it back with no `Defer` task at all;
`MCache.numcuts` counts exactly those (`Cut.mesh.cur() != null`) and `MCache.cutbuilt` (`// addon:`) asks it
of one cut, as a plain lookup that neither builds nor requests. ⚠️ A budget that reads absence from `cuts` as
"being built" therefore throttles work nobody is doing: it is the right test for what is IN FLIGHT only
while a dropped cut also loses its mesh, and a source that keeps its grids breaks that coupling — admit an
already-built cut for free and charge the budget for the rest.

**Gotcha — a built cut keeps what laying it again reads, and each `Model` is let go of only if nothing holds
it.** `MapMesh.build` ends in `clean()`, which drops every `data` entry whose `ConsHooks.clean()` answers
false — each `Model`, the `MeshBuf` its mesh was made from, every vertex, face and layer value of it an object —
and keeps the ones that answer true: `MapSurface` (every corner a `Surface.Vertex`, the corners of every face in
`fv`/`tv`, the normals), `Ridges` and `WaterTile.BottomData`. Those are what laying the cut's tiles again reads:
`makeol`/`makeolol` (overlays), `consgrid` (grid lines), `groundmod` (ground decals) and the flavor pass
(`Ridges.getrdesc`). Measured over recorded grids, a built cut is some 700 KB under the lightest ground settings
and 1.7 MB under the defaults; of that, the kept surface and tile data is a quarter at the lightest and a tenth
at the defaults, and the `Model`s are half to two thirds — **if anything holds them**. ⚠️ So hold a `Model`'s
finished mesh and material and never the `Model`: a list of `Model`s keeps every `MeshBuf` for as long as the
cut lives, which is what `MapMesh.parts()` (`// addon:`) avoids. Fork: an `MCache` built lean
(`MCache(Session, boolean)`, `// addon:`) has each cut let go of its `data` as soon as it is built
(`MapMesh.lean`) — the remembered ground's, whose cuts are drawn through a merged mesh, clicked through `flat`
and stood on through `getsurf(SurfaceID.map)`, whose surface reads the map's heights and not the cut. ⚠️ Nothing
may lay a lean cut again: `MapMesh.data` throws `IllegalStateException`, so an overlay, grid lines, a decal, a
flavor pass or a water bottom's surface (`WaterTile.BottomSurface`, a `SurfaceID.trn`) over such a cache fails
loudly instead of building over an unfinished surface.
